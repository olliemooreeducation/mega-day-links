📘 Mission Control Bio-Textbook (Year 8 Field Guide)

🦠 Chapter 1: Cells, Ingredients & Bioreactor Temperatures

In space biotechnology, we grow microscopic cells inside shiny tanks called Bioreactors to make medicines, oxygen, biofuel, and food. Every microbe has enzymes inside it. If the tank is too cold, reactions slow down. If it is too hot, the enzymes denature (lose their shape and stop working)!

Cell TypeIdeal TempRequired Ingredients (Broth)Oxygen / Light Rule
E. coli Bacteria (Prokaryote)37°C (Body temp)Glucose + Amino AcidsNeeds Aerobic (Oxygen ON) for fast growth & insulin making.
Baker's / Brewer's Yeast (Fungus)30°C (Warm)Sucrose Sugar + Yeast ExtractAnaerobic (No Oxygen) for Ethanol Biofuel; Aerobic (Oxygen ON) for biomass!
Spirulina Algae (Photosynthetic)25°C (Mild)CO2 + Mineral NitratesNeeds UV-Grow Light + CO2 (Makes oxygen via photosynthesis!).
Lactobacillus (Yogurt Bacteria)42°C (Cozy warm)Lactose Milk SugarAnaerobic (No Oxygen) to ferment milk sugar into lactic acid.
Thermus aquaticus (Extremophile)72°C (Super hot!)Sulfur Minerals + PeptoneAerobic (Oxygen ON); survives boiling volcanic vents!
Mammalian Stem Cells37°C (Body temp)Growth Serum + VitaminsAerobic + 5% CO2 Buffer (Fragile—has no cell wall!).
🔬 Year 8 Cell Tip: Bacteria are prokaryotes (no nucleus; their DNA floats in circular rings called plasmids). Yeast, Algae, and Mammalian cells are eukaryotes (DNA is stored safely inside a nucleus).

🧬 Chapter 2: DNA Structure, Base Pairing & Gene Splicing

DNA is shaped like a twisted ladder called a double helix. The rungs of the ladder are made of four chemical bases that always pair up following strict rules:

  • 🔴 Adenine (A) ALWAYS pairs with 🔵 Thymine (T)
  • 🟢 Cytosine (C) ALWAYS pairs with 🟡 Guanine (G)

To give bacteria a new superpower, genetic engineers cut open a bacterial DNA ring (a Plasmid) and paste in a new gene:

Molecular ToolWhat It Does in the LabWhen to Choose It
EcoRI EnzymeMolecular scissors that cut cleanly at GAATTC sequences.Used for Human Insulin & Hormone genes.
BamHI EnzymeMolecular scissors that cut at GGATCC sequences.Used for Bioluminescent (Glow) GFP & Radiation-Shield genes.
HindIII EnzymeMolecular scissors that cut at AAGCTT sequences.Used for Crop Frost/Drought Resistance & Plastic-Eating genes.
DNA LigaseMolecular Glue! Seals the sticky ends of DNA back together.Required in every plasmid splicing mission to finish the ring.

🔥 Chapter 3: PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction) – The DNA Photocopier

When astronauts find a tiny drop of DNA on a meteorite or airlock, they use a PCR Thermal Cycler to make millions of copies. It works in 3 repeating temperature steps:

PCR StepExact TemperatureWhat Happens to the DNA?
Step 1: Denaturation95°CIntense heat breaks the hydrogen bonds and unzips the double-stranded DNA into two single strands.
Step 2: Annealing55°CThe tube cools so short RNA/DNA Primers can lock onto the start of the target gene.
Step 3: Extension72°CTaq Polymerase (the heat-proof enzyme from hot-spring bacteria) builds the new DNA strand!
🧮 Doubling Math Rule: Every 1 cycle of PCR doubles the number of DNA copies!
• Start with 1 copy → 1 cycle = 2 → 2 cycles = 4 → 3 cycles = 8 → 4 cycles = 16 → 5 cycles = 32 copies!

⚡ Chapter 4: Gel Electrophoresis – Sorting DNA by Size

How do we see DNA fragments to solve a mystery or check a strain? We push DNA through a spongy jelly slab called an Agarose Gel using electricity!

  • 🔋 Charge Rule: DNA has a natural NEGATIVE (-) charge. Because opposites attract, DNA always swims away from the Negative (-) Electrode at the top wells and races toward the Positive (+) Electrode at the bottom!
  • 🏃 Speed & Size Rule: Smaller, shorter DNA pieces (fewer base pairs, like 100 bp) wiggle through the gel pores much faster and travel farther down than heavy, long pieces (like 900 bp).
🔎 Forensic Matching Tip: To match a mystery sample or culprit, look for the Lane whose glowing bands line up at the exact same horizontal heights (bp sizes) as the Evidence lane!

✂️ Chapter 5: CRISPR-Cas9 – Precision Gene Editing

CRISPR-Cas9 is like a GPS-guided pair of scissors that can fix a spelling mistake (mutation) inside a living cell's DNA.

  • 🧭 Guide RNA (gRNA): Cas9 needs a matching piece of RNA to find the target DNA. RNA uses the same pairing rules as DNA, EXCEPT there is NO Thymine (T) in RNA! Instead, RNA uses Uracil (U):
    • DNA A pairs with RNA U  |  DNA T pairs with RNA A  |  DNA C pairs with RNA G  |  DNA G pairs with RNA C
  • 🎯 PAM Site (NGG): Cas9 will only cut next to a 3-letter safety tag called a PAM sequence (ending in GG, such as AGG, TGG, or CGG).
